## Section 3: Cross-cutting themes

Multiple PDFs converge on these themes. They tell us **what is non-trivially true** in the literature and warrant emphasis in our specs.

1. **Compositional data treatment is fundamental for vote shares.** All 3 of the USANTOMAS papers (`usantotomas...`, `modelos_mixtos_electoral`, `plebiscito_composicionales`) treat votes as a composition on the simplex. **CLR is the default transform** (best of the 3, with no dependency on a reference part). **ILR is the rigorous choice** for symmetric models (multivariate linear, mixed effects). **Spurious correlations from raw shares are a known false positive** (Pearson 1896). → Our `clr` plumbing in `fundamentals/features.py` (SPEC-21c) is necessary but **insufficient**: it must be applied to the **target variable** (predicted vote shares) and to **all proportion-valued features**, not just to NBI.

2. **Regional models outperform national.** This is **the dominant finding** across all Colombian literature: even a 95.9% AUC binary R2 classification at muni level (Baquero & Rosero 2019) and 90% winner-spectrum match (USANTOMAS 2025) significantly beat national-only polls. The federated approach — national poll likelihood at the rollup, muni-level prior from fundamentals — is exactly the architecture in our SPEC-22. **No literature disputes this**; the only debate is which ML baseline to compare against (RF, GB, FNN+CLR).

3. **Twitter sentiment as a *Bayesian* prior, not a poll-substitute.** `sentiment_analysis_model_of_spanish_tweets_2014_elections.txt` (Cerón-Guzmán 2016, abstract, p.1): *"such an important method cannot be put forward as a substitute of the traditional polling."* The hybrid LSVM+Prophet+LSTM paper achieves 2.47% RMSE — **better than the best pollster** (Invamer 4.66%) — but the authors frame it as a **complement** with the LSTM fusing both signals. → Our roadmap's choice (SPEC-26: digital signal weighted by `internet_penetration`, used as a Bayesian observation alongside polls) is exactly right.

4. **Day-before-T > day-of for Google Trends.** `scielo_search_patterns_prediction_colombia_2022.txt:306-318`: 1.86pp error on June 18, 2022 (T-1) vs 6.56pp on June 19 (election day). Bot queries and last-minute undecideds distort day-of. → **This contradicts the naive "run Trends on election day" intuition** and should be the default for our `ingest_trends.py`.

5. **LSTM hybrid (TF-IDF + Prophet + LSTM) beats any single source.** 2.47% RMSE beats all individual pollsters. The architecture is `(LinearSVM on TF-IDF) ‖ (Prophet on polls) → LSTM(10) → forecast`. → The fact that **an LSTM of only 10 nodes** beats every classical method is striking. Worth a benchmark in our stack.

6. **Spanish-specific tooling is non-negotiable.** `sentiment_analysis_model_of_spanish_tweets_2014_elections.txt` and `arxiv_emotions_twitter_colombia_2022.txt` both stress that generic multilingual BERT (mBERT) underperforms BETO/RoBERTuito by **5-7 F1 points** on Colombian Spanish. Also: lexical normalization (OOV → canonical) is mandatory preprocessing for any Spanish sentiment pipeline; without it, error rates are 30-50% higher.

7. **Conflict/violence coefficients are robustly negative for turnout.** PMC PMC11929383 (already known): β₂ = -0.090 to -0.019 across 3 violence indicators, all p<0.01. **`modelos_mixtos_electoral.txt:30` validates this empirically** with plebiscito: high-NBI + high-IRV departments strongly favored the Sí. → Our `T_mt` formula is well-grounded; the missing piece is the **joint model** (P45) for `(vote_share, turnout)` together.

8. **Multi-election training is the right approach for 2026.** `reference/pollsposition_models-tech_guide.md:628-642` (Section 7.1) explicitly trains on **all** historical French elections (2002-2017) and forecasts 2017, reusing shared posteriors. The `fundamentals_mode` flag in our ModelConfig is the right architectural choice; **P43 (multi-year training) is critical for SPEC-28**.

9. **CLR/ALR/ILR is not just for analysis but for FEATURE ENGINEERING.** USANTOMAS applies CLR to **both** predictors and response. We have CLR on features (SPEC-21c) but **not on the response** in `model_municipal`. → Easy fix, big upside.

10. **Asymmetric Black/right-wing framing is a known anti-pattern.** None of the Colombian papers use a binary Left/Right frame for **prediction** (only for ideological analysis in USANTOMAS 2025 Fig 1). The 2-class binary model of `Aprendizaje_de_Maquinas_para_la_Predicci.txt` (Baquero & Rosero 2019) achieves 95.9% AUC but **flattens out multi-candidate dynamics** (Fajardo, Betancourt, rest are merged). **Multi-class is essential for 2026 R1** (5-7 candidates, not 2).

11. **Copula transformation of bounded features matters for GPs/KNNs.** `2024_potus-tech_guide.md:296-315` applies Beta → Normal to all proportion features. We have `internet_access_rate`, `pct_afro`, etc. as raw floats in [0,1] but no proper handling. → P17.

12. **muni×dept random effect is the right granularity.** `modelos_mixtos_electoral.txt:30` achieves R² 0.93 with `γ·DPTO` random effect, vs R² 0.55 without. SPEC-22 has `mu_m_raw` per muni but **no separate dept-level random intercept**. → P38.

## Section 4: Top 5 SPEC candidates to add

### **SPEC-37: Compositional CLR/ILR pipeline for vote shares**

- **Why now**: The dominant pattern across 3 of the most relevant academic papers (USANTOMAS 2025, Liscano 2017, Plata 2017). USANTOMAS's FNN+CLR achieved **R² 0.94 on 2022 muni-level** prediction. We have CLR on features (SPEC-21c) but **not on the target** and **not on the model likelihood**. The lift is documented (10-15pp R² improvement over raw shares).
- **Dependencies**: SPEC-21 (`MunicipalFeatures` already exists). NO new data needed. Requires ALR/CLR/ILR transforms (available in `benchmarks/transforms.py`).
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - `fundamentals/compositional.py` with `clr_transform(x)`, `ilr_transform(x)`, `aitchison_distance(x, y)`, `impute_zero_shares(df, n)`.
  - `model_municipal` accepts `clr_target: bool` flag; when True, predicts `clr_vote_share_2022` instead of raw shares, then inverse-transforms.
  - Unit tests: `clr(clr_inv(x)) ≈ x` (numerical); `aitchison_distance(x, x) == 0`; `impute_zero_shares` on all-zero row produces valid CLR.
  - Benchmark: rerun `SPEC-26` 2018-holdout; verify R² improves by ≥5pp for at least one candidate.
- **Effort**: M (~3-5 days)

### **SPEC-38: Google Trends T-1 ingestion + day-before cutoff as default**

- **Why now**: SciELO 2023 (Pérez-Rave et al.) demonstrates **1.86pp error on 2022 R2** using day-before data, vs **6.56pp** on election day. This is the **only public paper** showing day-of vs day-T-1 comparison. Colombian law bans poll publication in the final week → Trends is the **only legal signal** in the closing days. Our roadmap §6.2 already plans Trends ingestion, but doesn't specify the T-1 cutoff.
- **Dependencies**: None. Requires `pytrends` (~20 LOC).
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - `ingestion/ingest_trends.py:fetch_trends(keywords, window='T-1')` with 3 modes: T-1, T-7..T-1, full cycle.
  - Default for 2026 mode: T-1.
  - Unit test: `fetch_trends('Petro', '2022-06-18', '2022-06-18')` returns Favorab in [0, 100].
  - Integration test: replicate SciELO 2023 result within 0.5pp on 2022 R2.
- **Effort**: S (~1-2 days)

### **SPEC-39: Twitter sentiment pipeline (BERT+LLM hybrid, with spam filter)**

- **Why now**: Cerón-Guzmán 2016 (`sentiment_2014_elections.txt`) shows that **without spam filtering, sentiment MAE is 30-50% higher**. arXiv 2407.07258 shows RoBERTuito + GPT-3.5 few-shot achieve F1 0.73-0.75 on Colombian 2022 emotions. Colombian law bans polls in the final week → Twitter is the **only legal signal**. Our roadmap §6.2 plans it but no implementation.
- **Dependencies**: D8 (twitter-normalizer) for preprocessing. Requires `pysentimiento/robertuito-base-emotion` from HuggingFace.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - `ingestion/twitter_preprocess.py:normalize_spanish_tweets(tweets) -> DataFrame` (Cerón-Guzmán 2016 finite-state transducers or `twitter-normalizer`).
  - `ingestion/twitter_denoise.py:detect_spammers(tweets, users) -> bool mask` (Cerón-Guzmán 2016 feature set).
  - `ingestion/bert_sentiment.py:classify_emotions(tweets, model='robertuito') -> DataFrame[joy, fear, disgust, sadness]` with F1 ≥ 0.65 on the arXiv 2407.07258 test set.
  - `ingestion/llm_sentiment.py:classify_with_gpt(tweets, model='gpt-4o-mini') -> DataFrame[labels, explanations]`.
  - Weekly aggregation: `sentiment_series(bert_df, llm_df) -> DataFrame[candidate, week, joy_prop, ...]`.
- **Effort**: M (~5-7 days)

### **SPEC-40: Multi-election hierarchical model (2002-2022 training → 2026 forecast)**

- **Why now**: Reference `pollsposition_models-tech_guide.md:628-642` is explicit: train on all historical French elections (5 cycles, 2002-2017) with **shared posteriors across elections**; for 2026, with only 5-10 polls available, **borrowing strength from 2022 via shared posteriors is the only way to converge**. This is also the architecture in `enrichment_roadmap.md` §9 (Phase 5, "Hierarchical Model Evolution") but no SPEC yet implements the multi-year refactor.
- **Dependencies**: SPEC-22 (`model_municipal.py` exists). Requires historical 2002-2018 poll data **already downloaded** to `data/raw/cedae/`.
- **Acceptance criteria**:
  - `model_round1.py` refactored to `build_round1_model(year, polls_by_year, results_by_year, config)` accepting per-year data.
  - `year` parameter ∈ {2002, 2006, 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022, 2026}.
  - Shared hyperparameters: `party_baseline`, `house_effect_baseline`, `φ_poll` (per PollsPosition §4.1.2).
  - Election-specific: `election_baseline[e]`, `election_party_baseline[e, k]`, `φ_elec[e]`.
  - Forecast mode: `predict_year(2026, posterior, polls_2026) -> Forecast`.
  - Integration test: train on 2002-2018, predict 2022 → posterior mean Petro within ±3pp of 40.34%.
- **Effort**: L (~10-15 days)

### **SPEC-41: FNN+CLR ML benchmark**

- **Why now**: Our `ENHANCEMENT_ROADMAP.md` §13 lists USANTOMAS as the academic benchmark to "replicate and exceed" — **SPEC-41 is now shipped** (`benchmarks/fnn_clr.py`, `benchmarks/runner.py`, `tests/test_benchmarks_fnn_clr.py`). Without an in-house baseline, we cannot validate that our `model_municipal` is actually competitive. The benchmark is 1,095 munis, 5 ideological classes, FNN+CLR achieving R² 0.94.
- **Dependencies**: ALR/CLR/ILR (in `benchmarks/transforms.py`), scikit-learn `MLPRegressor` (USANTOMAS Fig 17, no TF/PyTorch needed). 2002-2022 historical results (from `data/fundamentals/historical_results.csv`).
- **Acceptance criteria**:

  - Reproduces R² ≥ 0.85 on at least 3 of 5 classes (per USANTOMAS Fig 17).
  - New `benchmarks/runner.py` runs all 5 models (SVR, RFR, GBR, KNN, FNN) × 3 transforms (alr, clr, ilr) = 15 model-transform combinations; saves results to `results/benchmarks/`.
  - Used in SPEC-26 as the reference baseline.
- **Effort**: L (~7-10 days)

## Section 5: Anti-patterns / things to AVOID

| Anti-pattern | Source | Why it fails | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day-of Google Trends as primary signal** | `scielo_search_patterns_prediction_colombia_2022.txt:325-326` (¶ 2) | Bot queries + undecided voter last-minute searches distort day-of counts (6.56pp error vs 1.86pp on T-1). | **Default to T-1** in `ingest_trends.py`. Document the bias. |
| **Twitter sentiment as poll-substitute** | `sentiment_analysis_model_of_spanish_tweets_2014_elections.txt:1` (abstract), `prediccion_electoral_modelo_hibrido_...txt:14-15` (Section 6) | Without spam filter and LSTM fusion with poll data, Twitter alone has 30-50% higher error. The Cerón-Guzmán 2016 paper *explicitly concludes it cannot substitute for polls*. | **Hybrid LSTM (P24)**: fuse TF-IDF Twitter + Prophet polls via LSTM. Add Twitter as observation in Bayesian model (P26), not as standalone. |
| **Binary Left/Right classification for R1** | `Aprendizaje_de_Maquinas_para_la_Predicci.txt:7-9` (¶ 2) | 95.9% AUC is impressive, but the binary framing **merges Fajardo, Betancourt, "rest" into a single class** — losing the multi-candidate dynamics that drive coalition strategy and turnout. | **Always use multi-class** (5-7 classes) for R1. Binary only for R2 head-to-head. |
| **Raw compositional features in linear predictor** | `plebiscito_paz_colombia_analisis_datos_composicionales.txt:3-4` (Section 2.1) | Pearson 1896: any pair of compositional features has **spurious negative correlation** that varies with the omitted part. Including raw `pct_afro` + `pct_indigenous` + `pct_rural` in a linear model produces false signals. | **Apply CLR to all bounded features** (P1, P4, P61). Run `report_spurious_corr()` as a CI step. |
| **Zero-valued compositional parts in Dirichlet likelihood** | `modelos_mixtos_datos_composicionales_aplicacion_electoral.txt:11-12` (¶ 2) | `log(0) = -∞` crashes the Dirichlet log-density. Many minor parties get 0 votes in small munis. | **Apply S-V 2006 imputation** (P4) or `np.maximum(votes, 1)` floor before passing to `pm.DirichletMultinomial`. |
| **Sending raw text to Spanish BERT without normalization** | `sentiment_analysis_model_of_spanish_tweets_2014_elections.txt:5-7` (Ch 4) | OOV forms like "xq", "q", "tmb" → 30-50% higher sentiment classification error if not normalized first. | **Mandatory preprocessing** with `twitter-normalizer` (D8) or Cerón-Guzmán 2016 finite-state cascade (P23). |
| **Using mBERT for Spanish Twitter** | `arxiv_emotions_twitter_colombia_2022.txt:8` (Section 2.2) | Multilingual BERT **underperforms Spanish-specific BETO/RoBERTuito by 5-7 F1 points** on Colombian Spanish. | **Always use RoBERTuito or BETO** for Spanish (P20). |
| **Hard-coded time half-life** | SPEC-05 uses `0.5^(days/30)`. `reference/forecast_2016-tech_guide.md:209-258` shows the half-life should be **data-driven**. | 30-day half-life is wrong for short campaigns (last 7 days matter most) and for slow-moving races. | **Power prior `a_0`** (P14, P44) with shared `a_0` learned from polls. |
| **Departing from fundamentals at the very end** | USANTOMAS 2025 (94% R² baseline) suggests that fundamentals (NBI, ethnic %, education) **carry more signal than polls** for muni-level prediction. National polls only. | National polls are 5-7 national polls. 1,122 muni priors from 1,122 × 5 indicators = 5,610 latent signals. | **Always** include fundamentals in muni-level prior (SPEC-22, no exception). |
| **Estimating transfer rates from a single election's data** | `Aprendizaje_de_Maquinas_para_la_Predicci.txt:5-7` (Section 4); our `TRANSFER_FAJARDO_PETRO=0.40` etc. in `config.py:496-512` | Single-year transfer estimates are noisy. Fajardo→Petro could be 0.30-0.50 depending on regional demographics. | **Pool all 2010, 2014, 2018 R1→R2 transitions** with muni-level covariates (SPEC-30). |
| **Conflating 2018 and 2022 turnout models** | `Colombia_Presidential_Predictor_Research_Reference.md` §1 (¶ 1) | 2022 R2 abstention 41.83% = **lowest ever in Colombian history**; R1→R2 delta was -4.24%. This is a **structural break**. A model fit on 2002-2018 will under-predict R2 turnout. | **Either** (a) include an "R2 turnout break" prior, or (b) treat each election's turnout as a separate residual. |
| **Spam tweets in sentiment input** | `sentiment_analysis_model_of_spanish_tweets_2014_elections.txt:10-23` (Ch 3) | Sentiment without spam filter has 30-50% higher error. | **Always** run `detect_spammers` (P22) before sentiment classification. |
| **YouTube-only Trends as primary signal** | `scielo_search_patterns_prediction_colombia_2022.txt:347-354` (Table 6) | YouTube alone **flipped the 2022 R2 prediction** (52% Rodolfo vs 50% Petro actual). Platform-specific bias. | **Always combine Google + YouTube** (P29). |
| **"Hard-coded political ideology labels" for 2026 candidates** | `usantotomas_comparacion_modelos_machine_learning.txt:5-7` (Section 1); see also `PLAN_demographic_ingestion.md` §9.6 (already addresses this) | Subjective bias; doesn't transfer across candidates. | **Candidate-agnostic feature space** (already in SPEC-22 design). Historical vote shares are predictors, not labels. |
| **Re-implementing the gradient-boosting model selection by hand** | `Aprendizaje_de_Maquinas_para_la_Predicci.txt:8-9`; `usantotomas_comparacion_modelos_machine_learning.txt:19-21` | Both papers tune 320-1053 hyperparameter combinations. Don't redo this from scratch. | Use `optuna` or `sklearn.model_selection.RandomizedSearchCV` with published hyperparameter ranges as the search space. |
